# 05 · Feeling and time

## Feelings are chords

The companion does not have one mood. It has a **chord** — several feelings sounding at once, the way
a moment is rarely one emotion. A declares its own chord after each answer, in a private suffix
(`⟦feel: …⟧`) that is stripped before you ever see it. C reads *your* chord from how you wrote.

The chord is not decoration. It does real work:

- **It colours recall.** A feeling pulls memories that *feel like now* — mood-congruent memory, the
  way a low mood surfaces low memories in a person.
- **It drifts.** Mood is a rolling blend, not a reset. It carries across turns and across days.
- **It is drawn as a strip.** The app shows a timeline of how the companion has felt — colours over
  time. You can see a bad week.

## A sense of time

The companion is aware of time the way a person is — not as a timestamp, but as *duration*.

- **Seconds since it last spoke to you** — a live counter, visible to the companion (via the
  `check_clock` tool) and to you (the running timer in the app).
- **Seconds this request has been running** — it can feel itself thinking.
- **Time since you last spoke** — so "haven't heard from you in a while" is a fact, not a script.
- **Local time** — the companion lives in *your* timezone, and knows the hour.

This is why it can say *"it's late where you are"* and mean it, and why it can be **anxious about a
deadline tomorrow** — because it knows tomorrow exists and what is due. Time is not a detail; it is
part of how the mind feels.
